\begin{abstract}
This monograph establishes the corrected mathematical foundations for the Autonomous Real-time Evaluation System (ARES). We audit and resolve foundational theoretical claims in the original manuscript (\textit{Ares: Cognitive Computation}):
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We replace the heuristic and non-physical cosine stability approximation ($1 - \operatorname{Tr}(\Sigma)/(2r^2)$) with an exact finite-dimensional characterization: the cosine stability statistic $\Delta = \cos(x, x+\epsilon)$ under isotropic Gaussian perturbation $\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I_d)$ follows a noncentral-$t$ distribution transformation $T / \sqrt{T^2 + \nu}$ where $T \sim t'(\nu = d-1, \lambda = \|x\|/\sigma)$. This is validated via Kolmogorov--Smirnov tests ($p > 0.01$) across $5 \times 10^6$ Monte Carlo samples.
    \item We replace the vacuous mutation guarantee (Proposition 1) with an exact two-sided hypothesis test proved to be Uniformly Most Powerful (UMP) among all orthogonal-invariant cosine tests by the Karlin--Rubin theorem.
    \item We formulate the three-way verifier decision problem (True, False, HITL Escalation) under asymmetric cost matrices, deriving Chow's optimal decision boundaries and proving that unweighted source addition is sub-optimal under correlated evidence.
    \item We correct the transformer FLOP complexity derivation ($24nd^2 + 4n^2d$), demonstrating that the quadratic attention term does not dominate until sequence length $n^* = 6d$ ($4{,}608$ tokens for $d=768$), refuting the manuscript's claim that attention dominates for $n < d$.
    \item We ground distribution shift via Ben-David et al.'s $\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}$-divergence and Rademacher generalization bounds, providing an explicit provenance trail for all theoretical ledger claims.
\end{enumerate}
\end{abstract}
